Master Data, Governance & Cataloging

Golden records with owners, survivorship rules and a catalog people actually trust: master data as engineering — match logic that is measurable, stewardship with SLAs, and duplicate rates that fall on a schedule instead of in a slide deck.

Every metric argument is secretly a master-data argument#

Match discipline, survivorship and stewardship under the numbers

Master data is the part of the estate everyone depends on and nobody owns: the customer that exists seven times, the product with three SKUs, the supplier with two bank accounts. The fix is unglamorous — matching rules with survivorship, stewardship workflows with SLAs, and a catalog that records ownership and lineage so people trust what they find.

Governance succeeds when it is wired into pipelines: dedup and survivorship run where data lands, quality SLAs gate promotion, and the catalog is the system of record for 'who owns this and can I use it for X'.

Below: the failure modes that keep master data contested, the reference architecture that makes golden records defensible, and the console that tracks trust as an engineering metric.

Four failure modes that keep master data contested#

Precision-blind matching, unsigned survivorship, broken merges, stewards without rights

Each of these survives a committee and dies in reconciliation — the countermeasures make matching measurable and stewardship contractual.

Golden records: survivorship, ownership, the defended merge#

Match, survivorship, catalog and steward workflow as one system

Match precision is designed, not hoped for: deterministic and fuzzy matchers with measured precision, survivorship rules signed by data owners, and a catalog where every golden record carries its steward and its lineage.

One customer record, resolved and defended#

The resolution path with the evidence it keeps

From source contributions through match scoring, survivorship application and the steward queue — including the merge audit trail that answers 'who decided these were the same customer'.

Duplicate rate, match precision, steward SLA adherence and catalog coverage — the four numbers that decide whether the catalog is trusted or quietly bypassed by spreadsheet shadows.

Every metric argument is a master-data argument#

Match discipline, survivorship and the merge audit trail — the foundations under the numbers

Metric arguments trace to master data more often than to math: two customer records, three product hierarchies, an org structure that changed mid-quarter. The governance mechanics make resolution systematic — the match engine runs probabilistic and rules-based matching with precision tuned per domain, survivorship rules are signed per attribute (which source wins when sources disagree, decided once, by the accountable owner), and golden records are one per real entity with the merge audit trail complete: every merge, survivor decision and steward override is reconstructable, which is what turns 'we combined them somehow' into evidence.

Stewardship is a workflow, not a meeting: owned queues with SLAs, domain stewards with decision rights documented, and quality gates in the pipelines so unmastered records cannot promote. The console reads trust as numbers — duplicate rates per domain, survivorship disputes, resolution SLA adherence — because master-data programs earn their budget by making the metric arguments disappear.

  • Tuned match discipline

    Precision per domain; false merges are costlier than misses.

  • Signed survivorship

    Attribute-level precedence decided once, by the accountable owner.

  • Complete merge audit trail

    Every merge and override reconstructable — evidence, not folklore.

  • Stewards with decision rights

    Owned queues, SLAs and documented authority per domain.

  • Why do metric arguments trace back to master data?

    Because two systems disagree about the customer, the product or the supplier behind the metric. Golden records with signed survivorship end the argument at the record level — the metric dispute becomes a data decision with an owner.

  • How are merges decided and defended?

    Every merge is proposed by the matching model, reviewed by the steward where confidence falls below the signed band, and recorded in the merge audit trail — a wrong merge is reversible, visible and attributable.

  • What is survivorship?

    The rule set that decides which source wins each attribute on a golden record — signed per domain, versioned, and visible in the console, so a contested attribute has a documented answer, not an opinion.

  • How do stewards actually work day to day?

    Through the steward workflow: queues of low-confidence matches, exception resolutions and ownership reviews. The console shows trust as a number per domain, so steward effort lands where the data is least trusted.

  • What does the governance office inherit?

    The matching stack, the golden-record plane, the steward workflow, the gates and the merge audit trail — governance operates on evidence per domain, not on policy documents.

Bring the two systems that disagree about your customers. The diagnostic will measure match precision on your real records — and price what one trusted golden record would cost.
