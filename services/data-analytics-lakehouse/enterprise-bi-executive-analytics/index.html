The spreadsheet shadow is a product of slow truth

Metric contracts, certification and the dispute ledger that retire the shadow

Enterprise BI & Executive Analytics is delivered by an Achala practice team that stays through industrialization. Achala builds lakehouses with data contracts: producers and consumers agree on schema and quality, breaks are caught at the boundary, and every table carries an owner and a freshness target. Decisions downstream become defensible. Who this is for: CDOs and finance leaders who need numbers both sides sign, and teams tired of disputed data.

The build is unglamorous: certify the datasets behind the top metrics, wire the semantic layer, instrument the dispute rate, and retire the shadow spreadsheets on evidence. The payoff is meetings that start from the same number.

The semantic layer is the peace treaty

Metric contracts, certification and the dispute ledger that ends the number wars

The spreadsheet shadow grows because truth is slow: when the certified number takes two weeks and a meeting, every analyst ships their own. The countermeasure is a semantic layer with metric contracts, one definition, one join, one owner per metric, and a certification board with quorum that blesses metrics into the certified set. The dispute tracker converts number wars into tickets: a disputed metric has a defined adjudication path, and the resolution updates the contract, so every dispute makes the layer stronger instead of more contested.

Executive trust follows the mechanics: the board pack is built from certified gold marts only (a written policy, not a preference), freshness is published per metric, and the shadow-spreadsheet census is repeated quarterly as the adoption metric, when the shadow shrinks, the layer is winning. The console reads trust as numbers: certified coverage, dispute rate, freshness adherence, because 'trust me' is not a metric.

  • Why does the spreadsheet shadow exist?

    Because certified truth was slow: when a metric takes two weeks and a meeting, the shadow spreadsheet wins. Metric contracts cut the time-to-trusted-answer, and the dispute ledger measures whether the shadow actually shrinks.

  • What is a metric contract?

    A definition with an owner: the metric’s logic, grain, filters and refresh are versioned in the semantic layer, certified through gates, lineage, tests, steward sign-off, and every dashboard consumes the contract, not a copy of it.

  • How are metric disputes handled?

    Through the dispute ledger: each dispute names the metric, the versions and the resolution. Resolved disputes amend the contract and re-certify. A falling dispute rate is the trust metric the console headlines.

  • Can executives still get one-off analyses?

    Yes, one-offs run against the same semantic layer, so even exploratory work inherits certified definitions. Where a metric does not exist yet, the request becomes a contract backlog item rather than a private formula.

  • What does the analytics office inherit?

    The semantic layer, the certification gates, the dispute ledger and the console, the office runs trust as a measured program, not as an etiquette plea.
