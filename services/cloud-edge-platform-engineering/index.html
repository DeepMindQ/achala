The estate is one system — reliability and economics together#

Why golden paths, SLOs and FinOps are engineered as a single operating surface

Estates drift because the three disciplines report to different rooms: platform teams ship golden paths nobody adopts, SRE teams publish SLOs that gate nothing, FinOps teams produce dashboards that change no decision. The domain design joins them, golden paths carry the SLO template and the tag schema by default, error budgets gate releases in CI rather than in meetings, and every workload's unit cost lands in a registry with a named owner. The console reads reliability and economics side by side, because the trade-off between them is the actual daily decision of estate management.

The engagement sequence follows the same logic: baseline the estate (tiers, unit costs, failure history), sign the SLO framework with business owners, deploy the golden paths with compliance built in, and let the FinOps engine allocate from day one, allocation creates accountability, and accountability is what makes every other practice stick. Gates at each step demand evidence: a tier without signed SLOs does not carry production traffic, a wave without retirement criteria does not start.

  • One operating surface

    Paths, SLOs and allocation join at the scaffold, not in three dashboards.

  • Budgets that gate

    Error-budget exhaustion freezes releases automatically, by CI.

  • Named owners for every unit cost

    Allocation creates the accountability that adoption requires.

  • Gates with evidence

    Unsigned SLOs carry no production traffic; waves without exits do not start.

  • We just signed a cloud contract — is this still relevant?

    Yes, the practices start with placement policy and unit economics on the estate you already run. Most programs find a meaningful share of spend on workloads whose tier, SLO and owner are unnamed; that finding alone usually funds the platform work.

  • One cloud or many?

    Placement by policy, not by preference: the architecture practice prices the operating cost of each posture. Multicloud is justified where regulation or resilience demands it; where it does not, the honest answer is one cloud with exit paths designed.

  • What exactly is a golden path?

    A versioned, supported route from repository to production that carries SLO templates, security defaults and the tag schema by default. Teams can leave the path with a written exception, adoption is measured, and the platform team answers for the path's own p95.

  • How is edge different from another cloud region?

    Sites fail silently, links partition, and fleets are heterogeneous, so the edge tier carries store-and-forward semantics, partition drills and site-class schemas the data center never needed. It is gated as its own estate, not as a cheap region.

  • Who owns unit cost after handover?

    Every workload's cost lands in the registry with a named owner; the FinOps review runs with finance quarterly, and error-budget and unit-cost trends are the two standing agenda items.

Bring the workload whose unit cost nobody can state and whose SLO nobody signed. The two-week diagnostic will baseline both, and price what golden paths would change on your estate.
