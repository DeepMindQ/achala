FinOps & Sustainable Computing

Cost and carbon as engineering inputs: unit economics per workload with allocation coverage that reaches the invoice, rightsizing with teeth, zombie estates hunted on schedule, and sustainability reported from meters, not multipliers.

Nobody spends someone else's money carefully — allocation comes first#

Tag enforcement, fair formulas and unit economics with named owners

Cloud cost fails as a monthly surprise. FinOps here is engineering: unit economics (cost per customer, per order, per model call) as first-class metrics, budgets wired into deployment paths, and rightsizing driven by measured utilization rather than quarterly panic.

Sustainability rides the same rails: carbon per unit of work, scheduling that follows clean-energy windows, and the waste ledger, idle GPUs, over-provisioned clusters, zombie environments, reported next to cost because they are the same problem.

Below: the failure modes that keep cloud bills foggy, the reference architecture of unit economics, and the console that trends cost and carbon together.

Allocation creates accountability#

From meters to unit economics, the discipline that turns cloud spend into managed cost

Cloud bills stay foggy because nobody is accountable for shared cost and nothing gates commitments. The discipline allocates first: tag enforcement makes untagged workloads un-deployable in CI, the allocation engine prices shared cost fairly (with the formula documented, because allocation disputes are formula disputes), and every workload lands in the unit-economics registry with a named owner and a business metric, cost per transaction, per shipment, per student, per claim. The console reads unit cost as the headline, because a 20% discount on a 40% oversized estate is not savings; a downward unit-cost curve is.

The waste ledger then makes the invisible visible: zombie storage, idle clusters, over-provisioned environments, listed monthly with owners and reclaim dates, reviewed in the same meeting as the budget gates. Commitment purchases are gated by forecast evidence and headroom analysis rather than vendor calendars, and the sustainability overlay carries the energy story: carbon per unit of work alongside dollars, because both are resources the estate spends.

  • Untaggable = undeployable

    Tag enforcement in CI; allocation starts at deploy time, not invoice time.

  • Unit cost as headline

    Cost per business unit of work, owner-named, trended monthly.

  • Waste ledger with owners

    Zombies listed, owned and reclaimed on a date, not shuffled into 'savings'.

  • Evidence-gated commitments

    Forecasts and headroom analysis gate purchases; vendor calendars do not.

  • How do you get engineering to care about cloud cost?

    Allocation creates accountability: every invoice line attributes to a named decision, and teams see unit cost for their workload, the number a product owner can actually move, rather than a monthly total nobody owns.

  • What about shared platform costs?

    Shared cost is allocated by a signed driver model, per call, per tenant, per run, reviewed quarterly. The defaults table names the driver per shared service and the deviation path when a driver stops being honest.

  • Can finance and engineering actually share one console?

    Yes, the console shows the same unit economics both sides read: finance sees invoice reconciliation, engineering sees cost per request and zombie count, and the review calendar is one meeting, not two.

  • How do commitments (RIs and savings plans) get decided?

    Commitments are release-gated: coverage targets, utilization evidence and zombie cleanup precede any commitment. The gates table names what must be proven before spend is locked for a year.

  • How is carbon measured credibly?

    Carbon is computed per meter with published emission factors and reported alongside cost per unit, batch at 410gCO2e versus support sessions at 1.2g makes the trade-offs visible and auditable.
