Detection coverage is a measurement — not a maturity score

Technique-level testing, purple-team truth and the anti-theater metrics

Threat Intelligence & Managed SOC is delivered by an Achala practice team that stays through industrialization. Achala implements security your auditors can walk alone: control tests that run continuously, findings filed automatically, frameworks mapped to ISO 27001, SOC 2, TISAX and NIS2. Security posture becomes something you show, not something you claim. Who this is for: CISOs and audit committees, and program owners facing TISAX, HIPAA or NIS2 scrutiny.

Managed SOC here means an operating rhythm, not a headcount: triage SLAs, escalation ladders, threat-intel feeds that turn into detections within days, and the post-incident loop that turns every incident into new coverage.

Metrics governance — the anti-theater layer

How the numbers stay honest

Every reported metric carries its computation: precision from closed-case truth, containment from first signal, coverage from executed tests, metrics theater is designed out, not discouraged.

  • Measured containment

    Detect-to-contain defined from first signal, reported

  • Intel conversion SLA

    Feed items become detections within 7 days or are dropped

  • Ledger honesty

    Gaps are published internally and worked as backlog

  • Rehearsal rule

    Playbooks used in anger must have been drilled first

Quarterly purple-team results are signed by both sides, red for what got through, blue for what caught it, and the delta against claimed coverage ships in the board pack, unedited.

Coverage is measured per technique

Attack-path mapping, purple-team truth and the anti-theater metrics layer

SOC programs drown when alerts are counts and maturity is a slide: the honest program measures coverage per attack technique, which MITRE-mapped detections fire, which were tested this month, which exist only in vendor documentation. Purple-team drills provide the truth: emulated techniques run against production telemetry, MTTD is measured on the emulation, and the miss list becomes the detection backlog with owners and dates. The SOC console reads speed and precision, MTTD, false-positive rate and coverage per technique, because those three numbers predict the incident the program will actually handle.

Response playbooks are rehearsed, versioned artifacts, not wiki pages: containment steps carry pre-approvals for the actions that need them (isolating a segment, revoking tokens at scale), and the metrics layer is deliberately anti-theater, it reports what the program cannot do as loudly as what it can, because a SOC that cannot say where it is blind is a SOC that will find out during an incident.

  • Technique-level coverage

    Every MITRE-mapped detection tested; the miss list drives the backlog.

  • Purple-team truth

    Emulations measure MTTD on production telemetry, monthly.

  • Pre-approved containment

    Isolation and revocation actions carry standing approvals.

  • Anti-theater metrics

    Blind spots reported as loudly as wins.

  • What does "detection coverage is a measurement" mean?

    A maturity score is an opinion; coverage is validated per attack technique by purple-team exercises, phishing, credential theft, cloud misconfig, insider paths, with the gaps named and priced on the console, not averaged away.

  • How do you stop alert fatigue?

    Precision is a release gate: a detection ships with a false-positive budget measured on your telemetry, and detections that burn the budget get re-tuned or retired. The SOC drowns when alert volume outruns validated precision.

  • How is threat intelligence actually used?

    Intel feeds the detection-engineering loop: relevant techniques map to hunts and detections, and the console tracks which intel produced which coverage change. Intelligence that changes nothing is retired.

  • What does containment rehearsal look like?

    Containment paths are rehearsed like drills: isolate host, revoke sessions, rotate secrets, restore identity. The signal-to-containment path is timed, and the gates require the rehearsal before a detection goes live.

  • What does SOC leadership inherit?

    The coverage ledger per technique, the console with speed and precision as headlines, the detection gates and the anti-theater metrics governance, leadership argues with measurements, not vendor decks.
