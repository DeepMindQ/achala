Your RTO is whatever your last rehearsal proved — nothing else

Immutable vaults, clean rooms and timed drills as the only honest recovery story

Cyber Resilience & Incident Recovery is delivered by an Achala practice team that stays through industrialization. Achala implements security your auditors can walk alone: control tests that run continuously, findings filed automatically, frameworks mapped to ISO 27001, SOC 2, TISAX and NIS2. Security posture becomes something you show, not something you claim. Who this is for: CISOs and audit committees, and program owners facing TISAX, HIPAA or NIS2 scrutiny.

The practice covers immutable backup architecture, restore orchestration by business service (not by server), incident command that has been rehearsed, and the recovery evidence regulators and insurers ask for afterward.

Below: the failure modes that make recovery fictional, the reference architecture of rehearsed resilience, and the console that reports drill results as facts.

Fictional recovery: the four failure modes behind it

Untested backups, shared vault identity, unmeasured restores, untrained command

Each of these is discovered during the real incident, which is the worst possible discovery context, the countermeasures surface them in rehearsals instead.

Immutable, isolated, rehearsed — the resilience plane

Snapshots, vault, clean room and command as one system

Three properties carry the architecture: backups immutable and isolated from the compromised estate, restores executed in clean rooms with integrity verification, and business-service RTOs rehearsed with the command structure, so the first real run is never the first run.

The RTO is whatever the last drill proved

Immutable vaults, clean-room restores and timed rehearsals, recovery as measured fact

Recovery plans are fictional until rehearsed: the runbook references a backup nobody tested, the vault shares identity with the domain the ransomware owns, and the RTO on the slide has never been measured. The discipline makes recovery a systems property, business services carry named RTO/RPO targets, snapshots are immutable and air-gapped, the vault sits on a separate identity plane (compromised domain credentials cannot reach it), and the clean-room restore rebuilds into a segregated network before anything touches production. Rehearsals are timed, per service tier, and the drill evidence, actual restore duration, is the only RTO anyone is allowed to state.

Incident command is trained like a fire drill: named roles, a communication plan with pre-drafted holding statements, and decision thresholds written while calm. The console reads drill results per business service; the review asks the hard question, which service's RTO slipped, and what changes before the next drill.

  • Drilled RTO only

    Recovery claims come from timed drills; slides do not count.

  • Vault on a separate plane

    Immutable, air-gapped, unreachable by compromised domain credentials.

  • Clean-room restore

    Rebuild segregated first; production touch is a decision, not a default.

  • Trained incident command

    Named roles and decision thresholds written while calm.

  • What does "your RTO is whatever your last rehearsal proved" mean?

    Exactly that: an RTO written in a document is an aspiration. The signed RTO stands only while the last drill achieved it with headroom, the console shows signed versus drill-achieved per service, and a missed drill invalidates the claim.

  • How do backups survive a ransomware attack with domain admin?

    Immutable, isolated: backup copies are immutable within a window and isolated from the production identity plane. The restore path assumes the attacker holds domain admin, which is the drill that matters.

  • How often do you rehearse?

    Per the drill calendar: every business service drills against its signed RTO on a cadence, with scenarios rotated, partial corruption, region loss, identity-plane compromise, and results posted to the console.

  • What does incident command get at handover?

    The drill calendar, the runbooks from real drills, the console and the gates, command shifts from improvising a recovery to executing the rehearsed one, with evidence the drill already produced.

  • Can you work with our existing backup estate?

    Yes, the integration surface names the backup estates and business services. Where immutability or isolation is missing, the defaults table names the deviation and the price of signing it.
