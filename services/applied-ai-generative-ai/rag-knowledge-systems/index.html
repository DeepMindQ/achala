Why corpus engineering decides the answer quality

Canonical registry, dedup and ownership, the unglamorous work behind every good answer

RAG & Enterprise Knowledge Systems is delivered by an Achala practice team that stays through industrialization. Achala ships applied AI with its evidence attached: evaluation harnesses, drift monitoring and measured deltas against your baseline. If a model does not beat the baseline, we say so — that honesty is why clients keep the programs running. Who this is for: product and business leaders with a P&L line to move, and CDOs consolidating AI governance.

The organizational parts bite harder than the technical ones: document estates duplicate across portals, the authoritative copy is rarely the most recent one, and permission models live in systems the search team has never been allowed to read. We build the contracts that fix that first, because every grounded answer downstream inherits their quality.

Everything below is build-order material: the failure modes we design against, the architecture that fixes them, and the gates a release clears before a user sees an answer.

The control surface — permission fidelity, trace discipline, egress policy

The three controls auditors ask about first

Controls that make the system auditable in regulated sectors: permission probing, egress scanning, trace retention and the evidence pack that ties each answer to a reviewable decision path.

  • Permission fidelity probe

    Synthetic principals query known-denied content on a schedule; any hit pages security

  • PII guard

    Egress scan on generated answers; block-and-flag on pattern hit with reviewer queue

  • Answer trace retention

    Full trace with citations and prompt version, retained to the regulatory clock

  • Prompt change control

    Prompt diffs reviewed and signed like code; unsigned prompts cannot deploy

Permission fidelity probes run continuously in staging and nightly in production shadows, any synthetic principal reading denied content pages security before a user could ever see it.

Permission fidelity is the hard part

ACL folding, corpus hygiene and the freshness contract, the three disciplines that decide enterprise RAG

Consumer RAG is a search problem; enterprise RAG is a permissions problem with search attached. The index must return only what the asker could have read, so ACLs fold into retrieval at query time, denied documents never enter candidate sets, and permission fidelity is tested with adversarial probes (privileged questions from unprivileged accounts) on every release, not just at launch. The canonical registry is the corpus-hygiene discipline: one URI per fact, superseded versions marked, ownership named, because duplicate and stale content is what makes retrieval confidently wrong.

The freshness contract closes the loop: every corpus carries a signed freshness SLA (how long a fact may serve before re-verification), the retrieval planner decomposes multi-hop questions rather than pretending one embedding finds them, and the eval suite is replayed on every index or prompt change. The operating console reads permission probes, freshness breaches and citation coverage as its headline numbers, those three decide whether the estate trusts the answers.

  • ACL-folded retrieval

    Denied documents never reach candidate sets; probes test fidelity every release.

  • Canonical registry

    One URI per fact, stale versions marked, owners named.

  • Signed freshness SLAs

    Corpus-level contracts; breaches surface on the console.

  • Multi-hop planning

    Decomposed queries instead of one-embedding answers.

  • How do you stop the assistant from answering beyond a user’s permissions?

    Permission fidelity is enforced at retrieval, not at the prompt: the index carries ACLs from the source systems, and a query only retrieves what the requesting user could have opened. Every answer traces to the documents it cited.

  • What does the assistant do when the corpus does not contain the answer?

    It refuses. Groundedness gates force a refusal when retrieval cannot support an answer with citations, a measured refusal is the correct output, and refusal rates are a console metric, not a failure to hide.

  • How do you keep answer quality from degrading as documents change?

    The eval suite is versioned and adversarial: each generation includes the queries that fooled the last one, and corpus changes re-run the suite before promotion. Quality is tracked per suite generation, which the chart shows.

  • Can this work with our existing document stores and ACLs?

    Yes, the connector layer ingests from your stores with their ACLs intact; corpus engineering (dedup, staleness, canonical docs) is the first workstream because it decides answer quality before any model choice does.

  • What does our team own at handover?

    The corpus pipeline, the eval suite, the console and the promotion gates. Your team can add a document family, re-run the suite and promote, we support the first two cycles, then step back.
