LLM Fine-Tuning & Model Operations

A tuning factory, not a notebook: decontaminated datasets, multi-task eval gates that block silent regressions, distillation that keeps quality while cutting serving cost, and canary rollout with a rehearsed rollback path.

Fine-tuning as a data-engineering program — the GPU is the smallest part#

Dataset quality, leakage discipline and eval matrices decide the outcome

Fine-tuning is a data engineering program that occasionally involves a GPU. The wins come from dataset construction — decontamination, label consistency, hard-negative mining — and the losses come from skipping the eval design and discovering regressions in front of users.

The operational half matters more each quarter: drift watch on live traffic, promotion gates with automatic rollback, distillation pipelines that keep the cheap model close to the expensive one. Tuned models are liabilities unless they are versioned, monitored and reversible.

Below: the failure modes that produce quietly bad models, the factory architecture that prevents them, and the gates a tuned model clears before it serves a real user.

Contamination to silent regressions — how tuning goes quietly bad#

Contamination, off-task regressions, unsigned lineage, no canary

None of these failures announce themselves in the demo — they surface weeks later in production, which is why each countermeasure is structural rather than procedural.

Dataset to canary: the tuning factory line#

Curate, decontaminate, train, evaluate, canary — as one pipeline

The factory has four hard boundaries: datasets are decontaminated before they enter training, every candidate passes the multi-task eval gate, promotion happens through canary with automatic rollback, and checkpoints are registered or they do not exist.

The promotion path — dataset to production traffic#

Gates from data freeze to estate-wide serving

From raw corpora through decontamination and labeling, into training and candidate eval, then canary exposure with the exact conditions that trigger rollback written into the pipeline itself.

The model-ops console for the tuned fleet#

Scores, costs and drift for every tuned candidate

Task accuracy against base, multi-task regressions, contamination rate, distillation retention and time-to-promotion — the five numbers that decide whether tuning is compounding or thrashing.

Model governance — dataset-to-canary lineage#

The audit trail from document to served weight

Model lineage is the governance product here: every production model traces to decontaminated dataset versions, eval results and approval records that a model-risk committee can walk without a guide.

  • Consent & provenance check

    Every training row traces to a consented source; untraced rows are rejected

  • Contamination sentinel

    Automatic overlap scan against holdout on every dataset version

  • Promotion quorum

    Model, eval and business owners sign before registry publish

  • Rollback guarantee

    Previous version stays warm for one full canary cycle plus a week

Contamination probes run against every dataset version: overlapping n-grams against eval sets are measured and reported — zero is the only passing number.

Decontamination is the first gate#

Dataset engineering as the program — leakage tests, task matrices and the promotion path

Tuned models fail quietly through contamination: eval questions leaking into training data make the golden set flatter than reality, and the regression appears only in production phrasing. The factory therefore runs decontamination as a hard gate — n-gram and embedding-similarity scans against every eval split, with provenance recorded per document — before any GPU hour is spent. The multi-task eval matrix then protects against the classic LoRA failure: gains on the target task that quietly degrade general capability, so every candidate runs the full matrix and regression on any off-task domain blocks promotion.

Method selection is budget-matched engineering, not fashion: LoRA for narrow style/format shifts, full tuning when behavior must move and data supports it, distillation when serving cost dominates. The registry carries signed dataset lineage — every training document traceable to its source and license class — and the promotion path ends in a canary ring before estate-wide serving, because the last mile of a tune is operational, not academic.

  • Hard decontamination gate

    n-gram + embedding scans against eval splits; provenance per document.

  • Multi-task regression matrix

    Off-task degradation blocks promotion, not just target-task gains.

  • Budget-matched methods

    LoRA, full or distillation chosen by behavior delta and serving cost.

  • Signed dataset lineage

    Every document traceable to source and license; audit-ready.

  • How do you prevent training-data contamination?

    Decontamination is the first gate: datasets are deduplicated against the eval suite and screened for benchmark leakage before a run starts. A tune that cannot prove a clean dataset does not reach the eval gates.

  • What stops a tune from quietly regressing on tasks we did not test?

    The eval suite is multi-task and versioned — a candidate must hold the floor on every task family, not just average well. Regressions below the floor block promotion, and the console shows the per-task deltas.

  • Is fine-tuning always better than prompting a frontier model?

    No — the chart is the argument: we size the smallest model whose quality holds the floor for the task, and distillation happens only where the eval deltas justify it. The factory prices quality against serving cost per task.

  • Can our team run the factory after handover?

    Yes — dataset lineage, eval harness, canary cadence and the promotion gates are documented and owned by your team at handover; we support the first two promotion cycles.

  • How do you handle regulated data in training sets?

    The data path inherits the platform’s controls: consent and retention are checked at dataset admission, lineage runs from source to canary, and the governance overlay names what an examiner can reconstruct.
