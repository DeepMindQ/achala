AI strategy as a portfolio problem — not a roadmap#

Priced ideas, tiered evidence bars and kill criteria beat promised timelines

AI strategy that works looks like portfolio management under uncertainty: a small number of funded bets with provable metrics, a platform decision made before the third rebuild, and a governance model that lets teams move without an approval queue eating the quarter.

The deliverable is not a deck. It is a funded first slice with a signed baseline, an architecture direction your engineers respect, a gate model your risk team accepts, and a portfolio triage that killed the weak ideas on evidence rather than politics.

Below is the operating machinery of the portfolio: failure modes, the reference architecture for the decision system itself, and the console the steering committee reads.

Decision governance — the audit trail of funded and killed ideas#

What the record shows, and who can read it

Decision lineage is the deliverable: every funded and killed idea carries its screen, baseline and verdict — the record that makes the next audit a reading exercise instead of an archaeology project.

  • Stop-loss rule

    Pre-agreed thresholds that stop spend without a political fight

  • Decision log

    Every gate decision recorded with evidence and dissents

  • Baseline integrity

    Signed baselines cannot be edited post hoc; revisions are new documents

  • Conflict-of-interest screen

    Buy/build calls record who benefits from each option

The stop-loss is signed at funding time: the conditions under which a funded slice is stopped are written before the first sprint, so the argument never happens after the money is spent.

The portfolio is priced at intake#

Expected-value discipline — why funded slices carry kill criteria and the ledger never closes

AI strategy fails as a roadmap because roadmaps promise; portfolios price. Every idea enters the intake with a value hypothesis and a cost class, gets a provability screen (what metric would move, measured how), and lands in a risk tier that dictates its evidence bar. Funded delivery slices are quarterly and metric-bound: each carries explicit kill criteria — the numbers that, unmet, stop the spend — and the KPI ledger tracks every funded idea from funding to retirement, so the steering committee argues from a ledger instead of from enthusiasm.

The platform direction memo is the quiet core of the engagement: buy, build or partner per capability, with the reasoning written down and revisited on dates, not vibes. The audit trail matters more than the memo — the record of what was funded, killed and why is the artifact boards, auditors and (eventually) regulators read, and it is generated continuously by the governance surface rather than reconstructed after the fact.

  • Priced at intake

    Value hypothesis + cost class + provability screen before anything is funded.

  • Risk-tiered evidence bars

    Class of service dictates gates; high-risk ideas carry supervisory-grade evidence.

  • Kill criteria at funding

    Every slice ships with the numbers that stop it — decided while calm.

  • A living KPI ledger

    Funded, killed and retired ideas tracked continuously; the audit trail is the asset.

  • How do you price an AI idea at intake?

    Every idea enters with an expected-value line: the value hypothesis, the data it depends on, the integration surface and the cost to prove it. Unpriced enthusiasm goes back to the sender — the screen is cheap, the provable slice is not.

  • What stops the portfolio funding only safe ideas?

    Risk tiers set the evidence bar, not the ambition: a regulated-customer-facing idea needs stronger gates than an internal draft assistant, but both are fundable — the tier is matched to the risk, not used to avoid it.

  • How do kill criteria actually get enforced?

    Funded slices carry kill criteria at funding time, and the gates read them: when the evidence at a gate says stop, the ledger records the stop and the reason. The platform reuse metric shows what survives into the estate.

  • Will this work with our existing PMO and stage-gate process?

    Yes — the rungs map onto stage gates: screen, tier, slice and scale each produce the artifact your PMO already expects, in your template, so AI investments stop living outside the corporate machinery.

  • What does the board actually see each quarter?

    The portfolio console: funded, killed and verdicts on one surface, with the ledger behind it. The record shows what was decided, on what evidence and by whom — readable, not slideware.
