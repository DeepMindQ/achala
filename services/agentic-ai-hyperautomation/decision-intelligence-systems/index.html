Decision Intelligence Systems

A decision system is judged by its worst defensible case#

Design for the decision a regulator will read, not the average case

Decision intelligence is where analytics stops advising and starts acting: credit lines, fraud holds, service credits, triage priorities. That step from report to action is governed differently, the decision needs a defined policy limit, a challenger, an explanation shape and an appeal path before it earns the right to act.

We build the envelope first: what the decision may consider, what it may not, what it must explain, when it must defer to a human, and how its quality is measured against a signed benchmark panel. Only then does the model inside get tuned.

Below: the failure modes that make decision models indefensible, the reference architecture that wraps them in challenge and explanation, and the console the risk committee reads.

The four defects that make a decision indefensible#

Unbounded autonomy, absent challengers, sampled explanations, drifting fairness

Each of these survives a demo and dies in a supervisory review, the countermeasures are the difference between a score and a decision.

Challenger panels around every decision — one governed service#

Champion, challenger, defined policy limit and explanation layer as one governed service

The architecture wraps the primary model in a challenger panel: agreement thresholds gate autonomy, disparate-impact monitors run continuously, and every output carries reason codes a human panel can audit line by line.

One decision, challenged and explained#

The lifecycle of a single output, from features to reason codes to appeal path

From feature assembly through primary and challenger scoring, agreement gating, reason-code generation and the defer-to-human path, including what happens when the panel disagrees.

The decision console the risk committee reads#

Agreement, drift and fairness as a standing agenda item

Panel agreement, disparate impact, reason-code coverage and deferral quality, four numbers that decide whether the model earns more autonomy or a mandatory human review.

The challenger scores every case, silently#

Champion-challenger economics and the disagreement triggers that keep autonomy honest

Every production decision is scored twice: the champion decides, the challenger disagrees silently. The disagreement rate is the most honest model-health metric available, it detects population drift months before accuracy metrics move, because challengers built on different features or different vintages fail in different places. Disagreement above threshold forces a review of the affected stratum before the next release, and the review is quantitative: which strata, which feature regime, which direction. This is the discipline that turns model risk management from an annual document into a weekly measurement.

The defined policy limit is the second half of the defense. The champion only acts inside bounds signed by the risk owner, exposure ceilings, eligibility overlays, mandatory referral classes, and the explanation layer emits reason codes on every output, not on the samples an auditor requests. Reason-code coverage is therefore 100% by construction, and the appeal path routes to a named reviewer with the decision's full context, which is what a supervisory examination actually asks to see.

  • Silent challenger on 100% of cases

    Disagreement rate as leading indicator; stratum-level review above threshold.

  • Signed policy envelope

    Exposure ceilings, eligibility overlays and referral classes bound the champion.

  • Reason codes by construction

    Coverage is structural, not sampled, examinable line by line.

  • Named-reviewer appeals

    The appeal path carries full decision context to an accountable human.

  • How do you keep model decisions explainable to regulators?

    Every decision carries reason codes with 100% coverage, a feature-provenance trail and the challenger’s dissent where one exists. The explanation layer is part of the governed service, not a bolt-on.

  • What happens when the challenger and champion disagree?

    Disagreement beyond signed tolerance routes the case to human review and triggers a calibration review. The disagreement ledger is itself a monitored asset, chronic dissent names the weaker model.

  • Can you work within our existing model-risk framework?

    Yes. The gates map to model-risk examination expectations, inventory, validation, monitoring, controls, and the evidence pack is produced in your framework’s vocabulary, ready for the examination file.

  • How is fairness actually monitored, not just claimed?

    Disparate-impact metrics run per protected class against signed tolerances. Drift beyond tolerance is a gate breach that blocks autonomy expansion, not a dashboard color.

  • Do you replace our models or wrap them?

    We wrap first: defined policy limit, challenger panel and explanation layer around your current model. Replacement happens only where the benchmark ledger shows a challenger earning it, quarter by quarter.
