The engagement ladder — four rungs, no leaps of faith

Diagnostic, provability sprint, delivery slice, industrialization, enter at the rung your evidence supports.

Work enters at the rung that matches the evidence you already hold. The ladder is deliberately short: the provability sprint exists so that nobody buys a large program on a hypothesis, and the diagnostic exists so the first conversation is about your estate rather than our slideware. Each rung ends with artifacts you keep, the documents are the exit criteria, not a courtesy.

The operating contract behind every service page

Signed baseline, named gates, patent-mapped methods, the clauses that hold across all eight domains.

Three clauses hold across all eight domains and every practice inside them. First, the baseline is signed by the metric owner before the build starts, no metric, no build. Second, releases clear gates whose evidence is checkable, not asserted, an auditor can replay the trail without asking us for anything. Third, the methods underneath a gate are published, counted and mapped.

  • Clause 1 — Signed baseline

    The metric owner signs before the build starts; the number, the source system and the measurement window are fixed in the document you keep.

  • Clause 2 — Named gates

    Every release clears gates whose evidence is checkable by replay, named gate owners, timestamps and artifacts, not assertions.

  • Clause 3 — Published methods

    The method families underneath each gate are counted and mapped on this site, read them before you talk to us, not after.

Where our patent-pending method families strengthen a gate, the service page names them and maps each family to the gate it reinforces, you can read the capability summary on the method map without asking for a briefing first. The patents protect how the gates bind; the contract protects what you can hold us to.

Questions buyers ask first

The four questions that decide the first meeting, answered before it.

  • Which domain owns our problem?

    Enter where the metric lives: a decision that gets better is Applied AI; work that stops leaking money is Agentic AI; data nobody trusts is Data & Lakehouse; proof your regulator accepts is Cybersecurity or Digital Trust. If the problem spans seams, most do, the hub architecture shows how the domains compose on one spine, and the briefing maps it for your estate.

  • What does the two-week diagnostic actually end with?

    A signed baseline, an estate map, a scored gate map for your controls and a build order with named owners. You keep the documents whichever way the engagement goes, that is deliberate, because the diagnostic is designed to be the cheapest way to price the truth.

  • Can we skip the ladder and start at build?

    Only when the evidence you already hold supports it: a verified baseline from an audit or a prior program can collapse the first rung. Nobody buys a large program on a hypothesis here, the provability sprint exists precisely so a fund-or-kill verdict arrives before a big commitment.

  • Where do the patented methods fit?

    Eight proprietary method families sit underneath the release gates. Where a family strengthens a gate, the service page names it and links the method map, so you can read what the method does without asking for a briefing first.

Bring the metric you own to a 45-minute practice briefing, we will map it to the domain that moves it, name the rung you should enter at, and price the two-week diagnostic that ends in a signed baseline for your estate.
