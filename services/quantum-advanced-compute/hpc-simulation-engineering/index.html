Most quantum conversations are really HPC conversations#

Benchmarked partitions, reproducible ensembles and estate economics

HPC & Simulation Engineering is delivered by an Achala practice team that stays through industrialization. Achala benchmarks every candidate method against a tuned classical solver on your data, and publishes the ledger — result, hardware, cost. Where quantum wins, you invest with confidence; where it does not, you know before the money is spent. Who this is for: strategy and innovation leaders evaluating emerging compute, and CFOs who want evidence before spend.

The practice covers architecture (CPU/GPU mix by workload), the solver library with versioned performance, workflow orchestration for ensembles, and the utilization discipline that separates an HPC asset from an HPC bill.

Cost per solve is the headline#

Benchmarked partitions, reproducible ensembles and the estate economics that fund science

HPC estates rot through folklore: partitions tuned by habit, solvers upgraded on vendor calendars, ensembles nobody can reproduce. The engineering disciplines them, partitions benchmarked per workload class with application benchmarks (not linpack alone), a solver library that is versioned and pinned with upgrade evidence, and ensemble orchestration that is seeded and reproducible: the same inputs produce the same ensemble, on demand, which is what makes simulation results citable.

Cost per solve is the headline metric because it is the number that funds the estate: per-solve cost trended by class, allocation decisions argued from it, and queue policies designed against it (a job that starves the partition for a weekend is a scheduling decision, not a right). The reproducibility overlay keeps the science honest, seeds archived, environments versioned, and the benchmark suite pinned so results compare across years rather than across press releases.

  • Application benchmarks, not linpack

    Per-class benchmarks that reflect the workloads actually run.

  • Pinned solver library

    Versioned with upgrade evidence; folklore replaced by record.

  • Reproducible ensembles

    Seeded, archived, re-runnable, results stay citable.

  • Cost per solve, trended

    The number that drives allocation and queue policy.

  • Why does HPC rot?

    Folklore tuning, unbenchmarked upgrades and scheduler drift: each change feels justified, none is measured. The estate is benchmarked per workload class, so a build earns its numbers, 80→94 on risk workloads, before users inherit it.

  • How do you benchmark honestly?

    Fixed benchmark suites per workload class, risk, CFD, ensembles, run on every candidate configuration, with variance reported. A score without variance is a rumor; the gates read the distribution.

  • Can you work with our existing cluster?

    Yes, the benchmarked estate overlays what exists: schedulers and storage enter through the integration surface, and the first build is a measurement pass that prices what tuning would buy.

  • How is reproducibility governed?

    The overlay versions solvers, inputs and environment per result; ensembles carry seeds and manifests so a verified result re-runs bit-for-bit. The reproducibility drill is a release gate.

  • What does computational engineering inherit?

    The benchmarked platform, the console with cost per solve as the headline, the gates and the reproducibility overlay, the team runs compute as an engineering asset, not a utility bill.
