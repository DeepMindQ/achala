A collaboration software vendor

Zero-trust multi-tenant architecture, enterprise security reviews passed in days instead of months and the top-100 enterprise deal barrier removed

Technology & SaaSCybersecurity & Zero-TrustGlobal Identity withheld · Baseline audited · Method under NDA

What we walked into#

A collaboration software vendor: Product companies feel platform gaps in churn and support cost, both visible in cohort data. The Achala program started with the release pipeline —. The baseline signed in week two made the cost visible in units the finance team already owns, and the engagement ran under the standard Achala charter — a named metric, a named control owner and gates that evidence every move.

What the baseline showed#

The baseline quarter counted the fear: enterprise security reviews averaging 11 weeks, the top-100 enterprise segment blocked on controls the product architecturally lacked, and engineering shipping features while security questions accumulated in a spreadsheet.

The multi-tenant design trusted the application boundary; enterprise buyers trusted nothing they couldn't inspect. The gap was architectural, and the sales team was carrying it.

What went into production#

We rebuilt the trust boundary as architecture: tenant isolation verified continuously with test tenants probing their own isolation, identity-first access with customer-controlled SSO and SCIM, admin action logging exposed to the customer's own SIEM, and a trust center publishing evidence, penetration test summaries, control mappings, architecture reviews, that sales can link. Reviews shortened because answers became inspectable.

The measured delta#

Transfer notes#

The publish-what-you-verify pattern transfers to any multi-tenant vendor; the isolation proofs were this product's architecture. Another vendor starts from its questionnaire spreadsheet, the diagnostic counts what it answers honestly.
